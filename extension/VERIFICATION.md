# Agent Lens 0.3.4 verification

Verified on 14 October 2026 with the unpacked extension in Chromium 141 (Playwright), disposable profiles. This file is a development record and is not part of the store package.

## Detector

- **0.3.4, Back after filtering:** a user's screen recording showed the page frozen about 5 s after Back from a stream to the filtered VALORANT directory; the bar then said "58 of 95 streams identified · 1314 under 50 viewers skipped", so about 1,400 cards were loaded. Twitch's code (`ScrollableTrigger`) loads the next page whenever its 500 px "load more" area after the card grid is in view, and re-checks after each page, up to five in a row. With most cards hidden that area never leaves the view. On live twitch.tv (signed out, so Twitch's later pages fail its integrity check but the requests still show the trigger) four short scrolls with every card hidden sent 8 load requests; with the area moved out of view, 0; moved back plus a scroll event, loading resumed. With Clove filtered, 1,000 minimum viewers and sorted by viewers: 0.3.3 sent 8 load requests while scrolling, 0.3.4 one (before its first pass) and then paused with "the rest are under 1000 viewers, not loaded". The page script itself is not the freeze: on a 600-card simulated Back (`tests/probe-scan.js --copies=20 --away=3000`) main-thread long tasks were 0.8–0.9 s without the extension and 1.0–1.2 s with it (0.27–0.4 s with a filter on, as hidden cards aren't laid out).
- **0.3.1, page performance (`tests/probe-scan.js`):** the captured directory multiplied to 150 cards, previews served locally; Twitch's "Back" simulated by throwing all cards away and rebuilding them. Before: passes over the page up to 106–287 ms (73 during the first read, 1.0 s in total); a rebuild cost one ~110 ms pass. CPU profile: mostly forced style recalculation (a `getComputedStyle` per new card between DOM writes), layout reads for ordering, and same-match grouping on every pass; the observer also fired on every text change. After: longest pass 25 ms, rebuild passes 19–26 ms (main-thread total on a rebuild about the same as without the extension), first read 0.47 s over 44 passes; nothing re-downloaded on rebuild. On live twitch.tv signed out (31 cards) the extension added no measurable long tasks before or after (`tests/probe-nav.js`).
- **0.3.1, quick check:** 95 same-moment pairs fetched at odd sizes (638×359 and 1918×1079, both fresh from Twitch's origin; pixel comparison confirmed the same moment). The small picture agreed with full HD on menus, agent select and broadcasts 95/95 and on the agent 94/95 (never another agent), but missed 3 combat reports and read only half the scores (3 wrongly), so it only decides menus, broadcasts and "no game" (no agent, no screen, fewer than two team portraits). It settled 34 of 95 streams; one stream with a visible agent was stopped there (looked at again a minute later). Estimated first look: 219 KB instead of 309 KB per stream (640×360 ≈ 46 KB, 1280×720 ≈ 130 KB, full HD ≈ 243 KB on average).
- **0.3.0, speed:** the template comparisons are a small matrix product per candidate patch and now run patch by patch (bit-identical to 0.2.0 on all 321 labelled previews at 1920×1080 and 1280×720), and in a WebAssembly SIMD kernel (`tools/kernel.wat`, 270 bytes): same agent and layout on all 321 at both sizes, scores within 2·10⁻⁶, 12–13 ms instead of 44–53 ms in Node. The kernel loads in the extension (e2e check); without it the JavaScript path runs. 1280×720 first: agents 97.8% vs 98.3% at full HD, never wrong. In Chromium on 40 live previews the whole 0.3.0 pipeline (with broadcast, team, mode checks) takes 54–66 ms per 1280×720 picture and 84–99 ms per full-HD one, against 115 ms for 0.2.0's full-HD first look.
- **0.3.0, teams:** portraits (the agents' square icons, already in `portraits.bin`) matched in the five slots on each side of the timer; the right-hand team's are mirrored (found by testing both on the labelled set: unmirrored left reads the streamer's own agent on their team in 138 of 178 previews, the other way 0). Accepted at 0.74 with a 0.15 lead over the next agent, chosen from zoomed slot sheets: empty slots (dead players, deathmatch bars) peaked at 0.72 with leads under 0.1. Own agent on their team in 139 of 177 at 1280×720; ~7 of 10 portraits read; a menu read one portrait before the reader was limited to in-match pictures. 16 ms per picture.
- **0.3.0, same match:** all pairs of streams in 8 captures (1,009 full-HD previews, ~15,000 pairs) compared. Shared agents are common (popular agents), so a link needs ≥3 shared on each side, agreeing scores, no differing full teams, and ≥9 shared without both scores. It links 6 distinct pairs (13 pair-captures), each checked by eye in their top bars: panzermausitv + zoovieh (4 captures), nobletofu + plainoletrey, dethseiya + kant0211, mea_07311 + roger9527, and opponents subroza vs zekken and nachoyunkie vs zozos_29. Two candidates with an unread score (kant0211/zzeekk7: 7–1 vs 4–6) were wrong, hence the stricter rule without scores.
- **0.3.0, broadcasts:** the ten player cards' thin coloured health bars at fixed places. 21 of 2,616 captured previews matched: Riot's own broadcasts (valorant_americas, valorant_br) and a watch party showing a VCT match (nuuhfps). 0.24 ms.
- **0.3.0, map and mode:** word templates from 22 agent select boxes (`tools/state-refs/select-boxes/`), letters cut out and size-normalised (undoing stretched resolutions). Leave-one-out: 34 right, 0 wrong, 4 not read (maps with one example: Fracture, Icebox, Summit; a partly covered box). The mode line is too small at 1280×720, so it comes from the full-HD picture that agent select gets anyway. Deathmatch: a clock of 2 minutes or more (rounds stop at 1:40); 8 such previews in the labelled set, all team deathmatch or deathmatch, none on gameplay previews.
- **0.2.0, speed:** profiling in Chromium showed agent matching dominated (176 ms of ~230 ms per preview; previews without a bar ~450 ms, as all 10 layouts were searched in full). The matcher now looks at half detail at every layout first, then runs the full check for the 6 likeliest agents at up to 3 layouts, in the usual layout order with the same early stop. On all 321 hand-labelled previews the agents **and layouts** are identical to 0.1.2 at 1920×1080 and 1280×720 (compared with the 0.1.2 detector run side by side), at 46 ms instead of 214 ms in Node. Round scores identical (39/52 and 33/52, none wrong). Pixels are copied out of the canvas only for the regions the checks read (`REGIONS` in `detector.js`); a unit test checks every fixture gives the same results from those regions as from the whole picture. The min/max filter used by the score reader and banner check is now van Herk/Gil-Werman (same output). End to end in Chromium on 40 live previews (18 with a bar): 125–145 ms per preview instead of 300–340 ms, slowest 210–250 ms instead of 760–860 ms, while also checking screens. Live run: 30 cards read in 13.7 s (0.1.2: 21 s).
- **0.2.0, screens:** templates cut from two reference crops (`tools/state-refs/`, `tools/build-state-data.py`): the client's top bar icons (menus) and the agent select role buttons, matched at four horizontal stretches; plus a round-banner test (three or more large white letters centred on the picture; a small "ROUND n ////// SIDE" line above means buy phase) and a combat-report test (the full-width red strip with the killer's name, ending at x 1899). On the labelled set plus 212 later previews used for tuning, icon-row scores on previews with an agent stay ≤ 0.41, while menus score 0.84–1 and agent select 0.62–1 (thresholds 0.62 and 0.58). All 115 combat-report detections were checked by eye: all real. Banner kind (buy vs result) right on 139 of 146 banners. Held out: 406 previews captured later, not used for tuning: 32 menu and 8 agent select detections all correct, 24 "dead" all real combat reports, no screen flagged on any of the 268 previews with an agent.
- **0.2.0, uncertain bars (`tests/eval-screens.js`, 1920×1080):** of 181 own-agent previews 156 count straight away and 22 are uncertain (combat report open or round result), shown as "Agent?" until confirmed; of 33 spectating previews 29 are recognised by the teammate picture and the remaining one with a bar is uncertain, so **none is taken as the streamer's own agent** (0.1.2: one). 47 of the 107 previews without a bar get a pill: 25 menus, 7 agent select, 12 dead, 2 round over, 1 buy phase.
- **0.1.2, dead or between rounds:** a user reported Grimm, alive and playing Sage, without a badge. The 1920×1080 preview had caught him dead at round end (FLAWLESS banner, combat report), when the game hides the ability bar; Twitch's CDN keeps that one frame for five minutes. Each image size is a separate CDN entry with its own frame taken at a different moment: the 1280×720 and 1600×900 frames at the same time showed him alive on Sage (0.90 and 0.96, well above the next agent). 0.1.2 reads a second size when the first frame has no bar, and looks again a minute later at sizes whose frame has been replaced (read from `cache-control`/`age`), never re-downloading a frame it has read. On 31 live VALORANT streams read at once: own agent found on 15 with 1920×1080 alone, 19 with 1920×1080 then 1280×720; adding 1600×900 found no more. The detector holds at the extra sizes on the hand-labelled set (`WIDTHS=1600,1280,1152 node tests/eval-own-agent.js`): 98.3% / 97.8% / 98.3% correct, 0 wrong, 0 agents named on the 107 non-gameplay previews.
- **0.1.1, spectating:** a user reported a Clove player shown as Jett. The preview showed a dead player watching a teammate: the game then shows the teammate's ability bar, with the teammate's picture bottom-left. Re-checking the hand-labelled set found 33 such previews among the 214 with an ability bar; 0.1.0 had counted those as correct, which they weren't for the streamer. 0.1.1 looks for the teammate's picture: 29 of 33 recognised at 1920×1080 (28 at 1280×720), none of the 181 own-agent previews mistaken for spectating (highest score 0.61 against a threshold of 0.68, lowest spectating 0.72), 6 ms per check. One spectating preview with the picture covered by chat was still taken as the streamer's own agent (two at 1280×720); the most-seen-in-the-match rule limits the effect.
- **Own agent, 0.1.1:** 178 of 181 alive-with-bar previews correct at 1920×1080 (98.3%), 177 at 1280×720; 0 wrong; 0 agents named on the 107 non-gameplay previews (`tests/eval-own-agent.js`).
- **Hand-labelled set:** 321 live previews from 109 VALORANT streams (three rounds, ~7 minutes apart, from the top, smallest and newest streams). Each was checked by eye: 214 show the ability bar (with the agent named), 107 don't (menus, agent select, Deathmatch, other games, overlays).
- **JavaScript detector, as shipped:** 97.2% of bar-visible previews named correctly at 1920×1080 and 96.7% at 1280×720; **0 wrong agents and 0 agents named on the 107 non-gameplay previews.** About 0.25 s per preview in V8.
- **Round score:** read on 39 of 52 hand-checked previews at 1920×1080 (33 at 1280×720), **none wrong**.
- Misses are custom stream layouts the detector doesn't search (a shrunk game picture placed elsewhere), heavily dimmed or covered icons, and death or respawn screens.

## Limits

- Translations haven't been reviewed by native speakers.
- Tested in Chromium, not branded Chrome or Edge.
- The detector knows the 29 agents available on 14 October 2026; new agents need `tools/build-agent-data.py`.
- Signed-out Twitch shows about 30 cards in a category; signed-in pages load more as you scroll, and those are read as they load.
